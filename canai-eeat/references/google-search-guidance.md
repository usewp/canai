# Google Search guidance used by this skill

Use these primary sources when auditing. They are living documentation, so check the current pages
when exact wording, a recent policy change, or a high-stakes conclusion matters.

- [Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- [Google Search's guidance on using generative AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)
- [Spam policies for Google web search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- [Search Quality Rater Guidelines overview](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/hsw-sqrg.pdf)

## Interpretive guardrails

- Google's systems aim to reward helpful, reliable content made for people rather than content made
  primarily to manipulate rankings.
- E-E-A-T means experience, expertise, authoritativeness, and trustworthiness. Trust is central, but
  a page need not demonstrate every component equally. E-E-A-T itself is not one specific ranking
  factor.
- Search quality raters evaluate system results; their individual ratings do not directly determine
  page rankings. Use the rater guidelines as a self-assessment lens, not a ranking formula.
- Topics that can materially affect health, financial stability, safety, or societal welfare receive
  greater emphasis on strong E-E-A-T. An editorial audit cannot substitute for qualified review.
- Generative AI can help research and structure original content. Automation becomes a problem when
  it produces many pages without user value or is used primarily to manipulate rankings.
- Creation-process or AI context can help when readers would reasonably wonder how content was made.
  Google advises considering disclosure in that context; it does not state that every AI-assisted
  page needs the same blanket notice.
- SEO can support people-first content. The warning is search-engine-first intent, not ordinary
  discoverability work.

## Questions grounded in the guidance

### Content and quality

- Does the page contribute original information, reporting, research, analysis, or meaningful added
  value instead of merely rewriting sources?
- Is it substantial enough for its purpose, accurate, carefully produced, and free from obvious
  factual or stylistic errors?
- Does the title accurately summarize the content without exaggeration or shock value?
- Would the intended audience find the page useful directly and leave with a satisfying answer?

### Who, How, and Why

- **Who:** Is authorship clear where readers expect it? Does a byline lead to relevant background?
- **How:** For reviews, tests, and comparisons, are the method, results, and evidence visible? When
  automation played a substantial role, would process context help the reader understand its useful
  contribution?
- **Why:** Is the primary purpose to help the intended reader, or to attract search visits regardless
  of reader value?

### Warning signs

- High-volume publishing across unrelated topics or extensive automation without added value.
- Summaries of other sources with little original contribution.
- Arbitrary target word counts, trend chasing outside the site's purpose, or entry into a topic
  without real knowledge solely for traffic.
- Promises to answer an unanswerable or unconfirmed question.
- Publication-date changes presented as freshness without substantive revision.
- A page that makes the reader search again for a complete or trustworthy answer.

## Evidence standard

Treat a criterion as demonstrated only when the page or supplied supporting material shows it.
Examples include named authorship, a linked bio, reproducible methodology, original photos or
screenshots, measured results, disclosed conflicts, a corrections path, and citations that actually
support the nearby claim. Their mere presence is not enough: judge relevance and credibility.

For external fact-checking, prefer current first-party documentation, official datasets, standards,
and original research. Record the source and distinguish verified facts from editorial inference.
